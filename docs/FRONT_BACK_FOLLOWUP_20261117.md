## Findings
1. The three page ends (Node vm, desktop Chrome, phone Chrome) are identical match for match: same winners, rounds and counters on all 240 seeds. The front end is deterministic across devices.
2. The page has no left-side bias. Left win share with equal tiers: page 1v1 49%, 2v2 56%, 3v3 52% (n = 35 / 25 / 27); mirror hero matches: 50% / 50% / 40% (n = 10 each, weak).
3. The 2v2 / 3v3 gap to the engine (left wins 61 and 58 percent on the engine, 45 and 44 on the page) is the strength of the hidden tier, not a side effect. Left win share by (right tier minus left tier), engine vs page:
   - left two steps weaker: 23% vs 9% (n = 22)
   - one step weaker: 35% vs 30% (n = 43)
   - equal: 45% vs 52% (n = 87)
   - one step stronger: 72% vs 47% (n = 60)
   - two steps stronger (hard vs easy): 96% vs 64% (n = 28)
   The engine's aim-noise tiers decide matches almost completely; the page AI levels separate players much less (hard beats easy 64 percent of the time).
4. Rounds per match agree within 25 percent in every size (1v1 9.2 vs 8.8, 2v2 12.3 vs 12.3, 3v3 17.5 vs 17.5 rounds); no errors, caps or no-winner matches on any end.

## What it means
- Skill gap deciding matches is normal (owner 2026-11-17), but a 64 percent win rate for hard against easy is soft: a clearly better player should win more often. Candidate for the strength tuning, not a defect.
- Per-hero flags (9 of 24 cells) rest on 10 matches per cell (standard error about 15 points); treat only repeated flags (bat, hag, hunchback, wizard in two sizes each) as leads, and re-run with more plays before changing a hero.
