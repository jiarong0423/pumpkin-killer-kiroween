## External evidence (web search 2026-11-17, standard mode, 4 queries; secondary sources, no single authoritative standard found)
- HUD: anchor to edges and corners, never absolute coordinates; apply the safe area only to must-see UI, let background and world fill the whole screen; test 16:9 and tall phones (19:9 2280x1080, 20:9). Sources: cursa.app mobile safe areas page, bugnet.io notch article, android developer docs (form factors).
- Scaling policy: Godot "expand + edge-anchored HUD" is called the most versatile for desktop plus mobile; "keep height" for strict landscape (height fixed, width grows). Pillarbox is the simple fallback. Unity CanvasScaler: Expand never smaller than the reference, Shrink crops. Our game is "keep height, widen up to 2.6, then bars", which matches the keep-height pattern.
- Touch targets: Apple 44 pt, Google 48 dp (secondary sources; confirm in the current HIG and Material docs); keep the drawn control small but enlarge the invisible hit area; bottom third of the screen is the natural thumb zone. At our scale 1 (932x430) the skill button is 44 css px, at stage 300 px high it was 31 px before the 0.82 floor: below the 44 rule.
- Hit boxes: the common advice is boxes tuned for gameplay, not equal to the art; a box LARGER than the sprite feels unfair, players like their own box smaller and the enemy's slightly larger; too much forgiveness feels floaty. Nothing specific to Worms or Angry Birds was found. Sources: critpoints.net hitbox essay, bugnet.io hitbox and hurtbox guide, tvtropes HitboxDissonance.
- Not found: any standard number for the share of the screen a HUD or dock should take. The dock share here (52 of 430 = 12 percent plus 6 px gap) is our own design number.
