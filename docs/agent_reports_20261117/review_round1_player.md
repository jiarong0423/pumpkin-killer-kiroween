## 2. Ten worst weaknesses, ranked
1. Team size only stretches the same game: one throw per team per round, so 3v3 = 3.1x the 1v1 length (19.9 v 6.4 rounds) [M ult/1.6, D]; 77 percent of plain 3v3 matches reach 20+ rounds [M plain]; [T] mean 16.9 (2v2) and 22.4 (3v3) against the target of 8-13 (gap A17). With the 12 s turn limit (TURN_SECONDS in shared constants) that is roughly 8-10 minutes per 3v3 match (my estimate, not measured).
2. Aim beats everything: expert aimer vs sloppy aimer wins 94.2 (1v1), 93.3 (2v2), 87.8 percent (3v3) [K]; the worst hero pairing is only 72/28. A weaker player has no catch-up tool in these numbers (crates/items were not simulated).
3. Snowball: the HP leader after 3 rounds wins 62-68 percent in team games and 73-76 percent in duels; after 5 rounds 70 (team) and 84 (duel) [M, D]. In a duel 51 percent of matches never change the HP leader [D ult/1.6]. A duel is effectively decided by who lands the first hit: first-kill = winner by definition, and 50 percent of duels have 0 lead changes.
4. 1v1 hero imbalance remains: skeleton 58.6 percent average (beats 5 heroes by 55+), hunchback 43.0 (loses to 6 of 7 by 45 or worse), wizard 44.7 (beats nobody by 55+) [J pooled]. [R] at 800 per pairing: skeleton 57.7, wizard 44.5, hunchback 46.1. E12's numbers reproduce; the older E1/E2 lines in GAP_QUEUE are stale.
5. Three engines disagree about the same hero. Duels, JS page-sim [J] vs Python referee [D ult/1.6]: bride 54 v 72 percent, wizard 45 v 66, knight 45 v 33, hag 52 v 37. Cause I could confirm: the Python engine hits for flat 34 with the bride wave (no 100/75/50 percent falloff; JS damage per hit-throw 23.2) [read of scripts/mcp_game_server.py `_apply_hit`/`projectile_hits`]. Also [J] plays only the bat ultimate, no skills, no crates. So the headline 1v1 balance does not describe the real game, and I cannot tell which engine the page matches.
6. Team balance is wilder than duel balance (Python engine): hero team-win share 40 (hunchback) to 70 (bride) in 3v3 [M ult/1.6]; flattens to 41-63 with human-like aim and 43-62 with sloppy aim [M ult/5, ult/9]. A bride on the team is a free 20-point swing in the engine.
7. First-mover edge in duels: 58.0 percent for the left side (range 50-71 by hero, bat 71 in seed 202) [J]. The page has a coin call (gap list E10 says the Python engine lacks it), I could not check it from a simulation.
8. Same-step hit ties go to the wrong unit: the official team log shows 32 ties in 3v3, the engine took the farther unit 9 times (28 percent) [T], against rule 4 "nearest to the path". 11 percent of 3v3 throws cross 2+ boxes, so this will show on screen.
9. Ultimates are very uneven: mean damage per ultimate (Python engine, incl. misses) bride 81.5, wizard 64.2, hunchback 35.7, reaper 32.2, bat 30.7, skeleton 24.8, hag 20.9 (hag's ultimate is below one normal hit of 24); 21 percent of ultimates deal 0 with expert aim, 33 percent with human aim [M ult/1.6, ult/5]. Ultimates cut match length only 10 percent (15.4 to 13.8 rounds, 2v2) so they do not change the game's shape.
10. The fence is almost irrelevant: 1v1 end-of-match wall HP averages 90 of 100 and it broke in 0 of 300 duels; in team games 52-54 left and it broke in 3-4 percent [M ult/5, 300 each]. Throws that hit it: 3.0 (hunchback) to 12.1 percent (knight) [J]. The map feature adds almost no decisions.

## 3. Replayability: match 2 vs match 10
- What changes each time: wind re-rolled every round (uniform -4..+4), random start x, random enemy hero. What does not: hero v hero outcome range. Fixed pairing over 300 seeds [F, noise 5]: skeleton v knight 66 percent for skeleton, rounds mean 11.0 sd 2.2 (7 distinct "stories" = winner, length band of 4 rounds, kills); wizard v bat 51 percent, 6.1 rounds sd 1.7 (5 stories); bride v hag 65 percent, 7.6 rounds sd 2.1 (7 stories). 3v3 fixed teams: 11-12 stories, rounds sd 3.7-4.9.
- Diversity numbers: 8 heroes; 5 of 8 within 45-55 percent in duels [J]; 6 of 8 within 45-55 percent in 3v3 with human-like aim [M ult/5]; hunchback is the one hero that stays under 45 percent in nearly every view (43.0 [J], 40-47 [M plain, ult/1.6]). Realistic pick pool for a player who wants to win: about 5-6 heroes in duels, 5 in the Python team engine.
- Skill growth is the main long-run engine (section 2.2): that is good for a 50-match habit, bad for a first-time player. The sims never model crates, heal/double/heavy skills, movement or the 5 other ultimates in 1v1, so they cannot show the variety these add.

## 4. Suggestions
Quick fix:
- Make the engine's same-step tie pick the nearest unit to the shooter (measured: 9 of 32 ties wrong [T]).
- Give the Python bride wave the 100/75/50 percent falloff the page uses (measured: 31 vs 23 damage per hit-throw; opinion on the exact fix). Then rerun [D] and [M].
- Reset the hunchback up by about 5 hp or 2 dmg and skeleton down by about 6 hp/1 dmg (measured gap 58.6 v 43.0; the exact numbers are opinion, retest with [J] 3 seeds).
Balance:
- Raise hag ultimate (mean 20.9, lower than a normal hit) and lower bride ultimate (81.5 mean, 4x hag) (measured).
- Team HP scale 0.61 / 0.45 from the designer paper would give about 8-13 rounds (opinion; I did not test it; measured target gap is 15-23 rounds).
Rules:
- Let one unit per team do more than throw (e.g. all living units throw at reduced damage, or a swap action) so 3v3 is a squad (opinion, based on measured 3.1x length with one throw per round).
- Add a catch-up rule: trailing team gets a crate/pity pick-up (measured leader-wins 62-78 percent, comeback 5-14 percent; opinion on the rule).
- Wall: let it matter (more HP damage per hit or a gap that opens) (measured 0 of 300 duels break it).
Presentation:
- Show "your shot passed through unit X" when a throw crosses two boxes (measured 11 percent of 3v3 throws, opinion on UI).
- Show an HP-lead/momentum line and the turn count (measured long 3v3 length, opinion).
