# N10 diagnosis, 2026-11-23 (read-only trace, no production file changed)

Question: why does `test_3v3_ultimate_over_page_judge_relay` end with `round N hash differs` (about 8 percent of runs, independent of CPU load, see stress_judge_relay_*).

## Method
`scripts/diag_judge_relay_desync.py` repeated the same scenario one run at a time through diagnostic copies of the two page seats that record every input of `RoundHash.hash`
(`scripts/diag_hash_probe.js`). 30 runs, 3 failures captured: `n10_desync_a_run19.json`, `run26.json`, `run30.json` (each holds the differing fields and the relay's full ordered log).

## What the traces show
| run | differing round | red seat unit that fired the ultimate | differing fields between the two pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 6 | `left_2` (reaper) | `right_0` hp 66.5 vs 69.8, `right_2` hp 104.5 vs 101.2 (3.3 hp moved between two victims) |
| 26 | 9 | `left_2` (reaper) | `right_0` hp 86.5 vs 69.6 and burn present on one page only, `right_1` hp 126.1 vs 143 and burn on the other page, `right_2` hp 53.6 vs 51.6 |
| 19 | 12 | `left_2` (reaper) | `right_0` dead on one page and alive (4.8 hp, burn:2) on the other, `right_2` hp 87.6 vs 99.5 |

In all three the relay log shows the same sequence: red `action kind=ultimate` from `left_2`, the white page answers with two `judge` messages (the victims it computed), then the hashes differ.
Positions (x, y) never differ; only hp and status words of the victims.
The five failures of the 60-round stress run were at rounds 6, 6, 9, 9, 12, and the 3 traces at 12, 6, 9: every one is a multiple of 3. The red seat's active unit rotates `left_0, left_1, left_2`, so a round divisible by 3 is exactly when `left_2` (the reaper) acts.

## Root cause
Red's team is bride, hunchback, reaper. The test fires the ultimate in whichever round red has three energy pips, so one run in three it is the **necromancer (reaper) ultimate**.
That ultimate launches three ghost flames from points on an orbit around the caster computed from the clock: `index.html` `orbitAt(k)` uses `performance.now()` (about line 2938). Each page runs its own clock (the headless seats a fake clock), so the two pages simulate three flames that start at different places, hit different victims, and apply damage and burn differently.
The page itself already knows this: `toggleUltimate` refuses the knight and the reaper in a room (`index.html` line 2126: "their ultimates use hidden placing or the clock: not in rooms yet").
The MCP bridge's guard is missing the reaper: `scripts/mcp_stdio_server.py` line 1989 refuses only `knight`, so an agent seat can still send a reaper ultimate into a room. That is the direct cause (a missing guard); the clock-dependent orbit is the root cause (the reaper ultimate is not room-safe).

## Consequence
Not a CPU-load or timing race. Any agent (or computer seat) that fires a reaper ultimate in a 3v3 room desyncs the pages with high probability. The human UI cannot reach it.

## Fix (smallest, consistent with the page's own rule)
1. `scripts/mcp_stdio_server.py` `PageJudgeRelay`: refuse the reaper's ultimate in a room, like the knight's.
2. `scripts/test_page_judge_relay.py`: the 3v3 test must throw normally while the reaper is the active unit and fire the ultimate with another unit; add a test that the refusal happens.
Not done here (needs the owner): making the reaper ultimate room-safe (flame start taken from the action instead of the clock) is a page change to `index.html`.
Not checked: whether the headless computer seat could fire a reaper ultimate in a hybrid room (its team in the test has no reaper).
